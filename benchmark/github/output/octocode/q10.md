## Answer

- **honojs/hono `src/`**: 10 subdirectories at the top level — `adapter`, `client`, `helper`, `jsx`, `middleware`, `preset`, `request`, `router`, `utils`, `validator`.
- **expressjs/express `lib/`**: 0 subdirectories at the top level — only 6 flat files: `application.js`, `express.js`, `request.js`, `response.js`, `utils.js`, `view.js`.
- **Architectural interpretation**:
  - **Hono** has a deep, modular, feature-organized structure: 10 subdirectories reflect explicit capability domains (`middleware`, `router`, `jsx`, `client`, `validator`, etc.). This reveals a **batteries-included, plugin-first** design built for edge runtimes, with each capability isolated in its own subtree and tree-shakeable.
  - **Express** is structurally minimalist: everything fits in 6 flat files representing the core MVC concepts (`application`, `request`, `response`, `view`). This reflects a **thin-core, minimal-surface** design philosophy where extensibility is entirely delegated to third-party middleware — the framework itself has almost no built-in features beyond routing and request/response abstraction.
  - The naming difference (`src/` vs `lib/`) also reveals era: `lib/` is a CommonJS/Node.js convention from the early 2010s; `src/` with TypeScript indicates a modern compiled source layout.
